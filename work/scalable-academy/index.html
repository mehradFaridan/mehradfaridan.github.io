Towards Scalable Remote Hands-on Education: Evaluating a Multi-Student Pointcloud Interface for Massage Therapy Education

AHs 2026 Poster· IEEE VR 2026 Workshop (VR-HSA)Virtual realityPoint cloudsRemote education

Mehrad Faridan*, Takashige Suzuki*, Takafumi Kanaya, Ryo Suzuki, Hideaki Kuzuoka* Equal contribution

PaperPDF

Hands-on skills like massage therapy are taught through a cycle of demonstration, practice and feedback, which depends on an instructor watching each student closely. That is hard to do remotely, and hard to scale. Scalable Academy lets one instructor monitor a whole classroom in VR: Azure Kinect depth cameras capture each student as a live 3D pointcloud, viewed on a Meta Quest 3. Instructors switch between two views: Supervisory Scale, which shows up to six students at once for quick sweeps of the room, and Observational Detail, which shows two students as fused multi-camera pointclouds that can be inspected from any angle. In a study with eight domain experts (five instructors and three teaching assistants from a vocational massage therapy program), the bottleneck of instruction moved from the physical load of moving around the classroom to the cognitive load of managing attention, and a model of their selection times predicts that one instructor could monitor up to 15 students. A related version, “Scalable Academy: Pointcloud Interface for Remote Hands-on Classroom”, was presented at the IEEE VR 2026 VR-HSA workshop.

ChameleonControl: Teleoperating Real Human Surrogates through Mixed Reality Gestural Guidance for Remote Hands-on Classrooms

CHI 2023Mixed realityTelepresenceRemote education

Mehrad Faridan, Bheesha Kumari, Ryo Suzuki

PaperPDFVideo

Remote video lessons work poorly for hands-on skills, because students need to see a technique performed on a real body or object in the room. ChameleonControl instead lets a remote instructor teleoperate a real person in the classroom. The instructor’s tracked hands are overlaid in the local surrogate’s mixed reality headset, and the surrogate matches them with their own hands while the instructor talks through the lesson, so the class watches a live, physical demonstration. We deployed the system in physiotherapy classrooms and explored mechanical assembly, sign language and cooking lessons. Across three studies with 53 participants, it increased engagement and the sense of co-presence compared to conventional remote instruction.

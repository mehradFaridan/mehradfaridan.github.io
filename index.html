Hi, I’m Mehrad.

I’m a mixed reality researcher and engineer based in Calgary. I’m finishing my BSc in Computer Science at the University of Calgary, where I started doing research with Prof. Ryo Suzuki. Since then I’ve spent over four years as research lead for mixed reality at MaKami College, and a year as a visiting researcher with Prof. Hideaki Kuzuoka at the University of Tokyo.

I build mixed reality systems that help people teach and learn physical, hands-on skills, especially at a distance. My work combines interaction techniques with live 3D capture, robots and haptics, and I test these systems with real instructors and learners.

I’m applying to MSc programs for Fall 2027, and I’m open to research and XR engineering roles.

Selected work

From watching an expert to doing it with your own hands.

Teaching hands-on skills remotely

Massage, physiotherapy and other hands-on trades are taught in person. These systems let an instructor see, guide and reach students who aren’t in the room.

ChameleonControl
video coming soon
CHI 2023First author

ChameleonControl

A remote instructor guides a real person in the classroom through mixed reality gestures, so that person’s hands carry out the instructor’s demonstration. Deployed in a physiotherapy classroom and evaluated with 53 participants across three studies.

Scalable Academy
video coming soon
AHs 2026 · IEEE VR 2026 Workshop

Scalable Academy

A VR view that streams live 3D point clouds of up to six students at once. In a study with eight massage-therapy instructors, they projected that one instructor could watch over up to 15 students.

HoloBots
video coming soon
UIST 2023

HoloBots

Mobile robots give a remote person’s hologram a physical presence, so collaborators in different places can touch and move the same real objects.

Turning instructions into guidance

Most how-to knowledge lives in flat videos and conversation. These projects bring it into the space where the work happens.

Video2MR

Video2MR

Extracts motion from ordinary 2D tutorial videos and turns it into 3D mixed reality instructions you can follow in your own space.

PHINIX

An Intel-funded AI wearable that helps blind and low-vision people navigate, read text and recognize faces.

My part: designed the audio and voice interface, wrote the module that prioritizes what the device tells the user, and prototyped parts for the haptic bracelet.
